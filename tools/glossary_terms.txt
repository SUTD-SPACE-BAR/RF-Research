# ---------------------------------------------------------------- decibels and units
dB ‖ decibel ‖ Ten times the base-10 logarithm of a power ratio. It turns the multiplications of a link or noise budget into additions; a voltage ratio uses 20 log because power goes as voltage squared. ‖ 10 log₁₀(P₂/P₁) = 20 log₁₀(V₂/V₁) (same impedance); 3 dB ≈ ×2, 10 dB = ×10 ‖ 00/decibels-and-log-units
dBm ‖ decibels relative to 1 milliwatt ‖ Absolute power on a log scale, referenced to 1 mW. The KB's default unit for signal power. ‖ dBm = 10 log₁₀(P/1 mW); 0 dBm = 1 mW, 30 dBm = 1 W, 33 dBm = 2 W ‖ 00/decibels-and-log-units
dBW ‖ decibels relative to 1 watt ‖ Absolute power referenced to 1 W; used in satellite and GNSS specifications. ‖ dBW = dBm − 30; GPS L1 C/A minimum −158.5 dBW = −128.5 dBm ‖ 00/decibels-and-log-units
dBi ‖ decibels relative to an isotropic radiator ‖ Antenna gain compared with an ideal antenna that radiates equally in all directions. The KB quotes gain in dBi unless it says otherwise. ‖ half-wave dipole 2.15 dBi; dBi = dBd + 2.15 ‖ 08/antenna-fundamentals
dBd ‖ decibels relative to a half-wave dipole ‖ Antenna gain compared with a lossless half-wave dipole. Common in amateur and broadcast specs, where it makes numbers look 2.15 dB smaller than dBi. ‖ dBd = dBi − 2.15 ‖ 08/antenna-fundamentals
dBic ‖ decibels relative to an isotropic circularly polarised antenna ‖ Gain of a circularly polarised antenna measured with a matching circular reference. A linear antenna receiving the same CP wave sees 3 dB less. ‖ dBic (CP) ≈ dBi (linear) + 3 dB for the same CP wave ‖ 08/antenna-polarization-and-circular-polarization, 00/canonical-values-and-conventions
dBc ‖ decibels relative to the carrier ‖ Level of a spur, harmonic or sideband relative to the main carrier power. Negative numbers mean below the carrier. ‖ harmonic at −40 dBc is 10⁴ times weaker than the carrier ‖ 00/decibels-and-log-units
dBc/Hz ‖ decibels relative to the carrier per hertz ‖ Unit of single-sideband phase noise: noise power in a 1 Hz band at a given offset from the carrier, relative to the carrier. ‖ 𝓛(f) e.g. −100 dBc/Hz at 10 kHz offset; integrate over offset to get jitter ‖ 07/phase-noise-and-jitter
dBFS ‖ decibels relative to full scale ‖ Signal level relative to the largest signal an ADC or DAC can represent (0 dBFS = full-scale sine). ‖ an ideal 12-bit ADC: SQNR = 74 dB, noise floor ≈ −74 dBFS in the Nyquist band ‖ 05/data-converters-adc-dac
dBFS/Hz ‖ dBFS per hertz ‖ Noise spectral density of a data converter relative to full scale, in a 1 Hz band. Used to compare ADCs at different sample rates. ‖ NSD = −SNR − 10 log₁₀(fs/2) dBFS/Hz ‖ 05/data-converters-adc-dac ‖ NSD
dBµV ‖ decibels relative to 1 microvolt ‖ Voltage level on a log scale, common in EMC receivers and in receiver sensitivity specs. ‖ in 50 Ω: dBµV = dBm + 107 ‖ 00/decibels-and-log-units ‖ dBuV
dBµV/m ‖ decibels relative to 1 microvolt per metre ‖ Electric-field strength on a log scale, used by EMC limits and field-strength regulations. ‖ E[dBµV/m] = P[dBm] + 107 + AF[dB/m] (antenna factor); far field E = √(30·EIRP)/d ‖ 02/electromagnetic-units-and-constants ‖ dBuV/m
dBsm ‖ decibels relative to one square metre ‖ Radar cross-section on a log scale. ‖ 1 m² = 0 dBsm; person ≈ −5 dBsm (mm-wave), small drone ≈ −20 to −5 dBsm ‖ 11/radar-cross-section
dB-Hz ‖ decibel-hertz ‖ Unit of carrier-to-noise-density ratio C/N0: signal power divided by noise power in 1 Hz. Bandwidth-independent, so it is the standard GNSS signal-quality number. ‖ open-sky GNSS 40–50 dB-Hz (≥ 45 healthy, < 35 fault) ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ dBHz
dBm/Hz ‖ dBm per hertz ‖ Power spectral density on a log scale; the natural unit for noise floors. ‖ thermal noise kT0 = −174 dBm/Hz at 290 K ‖ 07/thermal-noise-and-noise-temperature
dBm/MHz ‖ dBm per megahertz ‖ Power spectral density in a 1 MHz band; used in UWB and radar regulations. ‖ 77–81 GHz SG exemption: mean ≤ −3 dBm/MHz ‖ 19/singapore-imda-rules
dB/m ‖ decibels per metre ‖ Attenuation per unit length of a cable or transmission line. ‖ RG-58 ≈ 0.29 dB/m, RG-316 ≈ 0.72 dB/m at 433 MHz ‖ 03/coax-and-cables
dB/km ‖ decibels per kilometre ‖ Specific attenuation of a propagation path (gases, rain). ‖ oxygen at 60 GHz ≈ 14.7 dB/km (sea level) ‖ 09/atmospheric-rain-attenuation
neper ‖ natural-log unit of ratio ‖ Logarithmic unit using natural logs of amplitude; attenuation constants α in transmission-line and wave equations come out in Np/m. ‖ 1 Np = 8.686 dB ‖ 00/decibels-and-log-units ‖ Np
ppm ‖ parts per million ‖ Fractional error of a frequency (or a component value) in millionths. ‖ 1 ppm at 433 MHz = 433 Hz; at 915 MHz = 915 Hz ‖ 04/crystals-and-resonators
MS/s ‖ mega-samples per second ‖ Sample rate of an ADC, DAC or SDR stream. For complex IQ samples, the usable bandwidth equals the sample rate; for real samples it is half. ‖ RTL-SDR stable ≈ 2.4 MS/s ‖ 14/sampling-aliasing-bandpass-sampling ‖ Msps, MSPS, GS/s
SI ‖ Système international d'unités (International System of Units) ‖ The metric unit system; since 2019 its constants (c, h, e, k) are exact by definition, and μ0 became a measured quantity. Also used as "signal integrity" on some pages — see the ambiguous-acronyms section. ‖ k = 1.380649×10⁻²³ J/K exact; c = 299 792 458 m/s ‖ 02/electromagnetic-units-and-constants
CODATA ‖ Committee on Data for Science and Technology ‖ The body whose periodic least-squares adjustment gives the recommended values of physical constants; the KB uses CODATA 2022. ‖ η0 = 376.730 Ω; ε0 = 8.8541878188×10⁻¹² F/m ‖ 02/electromagnetic-units-and-constants
# ---------------------------------------------------------------- maths
phasor ‖ — ‖ A complex number whose magnitude and angle stand for the amplitude and phase of a sinusoid at one frequency, so that derivatives become multiplication by jω. The KB uses peak phasors with the e^{jωt} convention. ‖ v(t) = Re{V e^{jωt}}; P = ½Re{VI*} with peak phasors ‖ 00/complex-numbers-and-phasors
j ‖ imaginary unit ‖ The square root of −1, written j in engineering so that i can stay "current". ‖ j² = −1; e^{jθ} = cos θ + j sin θ ‖ 00/complex-numbers-and-phasors
Euler's formula ‖ — ‖ The identity linking complex exponentials to sines and cosines; the basis of phasors, the Fourier transform and IQ signals. ‖ e^{jθ} = cos θ + j sin θ ‖ 00/complex-numbers-and-phasors
Fourier transform ‖ — ‖ Splits a signal into its sinusoidal frequency components; the link between time-domain waveforms and spectra (and between an antenna aperture and its far-field pattern). ‖ X(f) = ∫x(t)e^{−j2πft}dt ‖ 00/fourier-series-and-transform
DFT ‖ discrete Fourier transform ‖ Fourier transform of N samples, giving N frequency bins spaced fs/N apart. ‖ X[k] = Σ x[n]e^{−j2πkn/N}; bin spacing fs/N ‖ 00/fourier-series-and-transform
DTFT ‖ discrete-time Fourier transform ‖ Fourier transform of an infinite sampled sequence; a continuous, periodic function of frequency. The DFT samples it. ‖ period fs in frequency ‖ 00/z-transform-and-discrete-time-systems
FFT ‖ fast Fourier transform ‖ Any fast algorithm for the DFT; cost grows as N log N instead of N². The workhorse of SDR spectra, FMCW range processing and LoRa demodulation. ‖ N = 1024: ≈ 10⁴ instead of 10⁶ operations ‖ 14/fft-spectral-analysis-windows
IFFT ‖ inverse fast Fourier transform ‖ The FFT run backwards: builds a time signal from frequency bins. An OFDM transmitter is essentially an IFFT. ‖ — ‖ 10/ofdm
STFT ‖ short-time Fourier transform ‖ FFTs of successive short windows of a signal; the maths behind a spectrogram or waterfall. ‖ time resolution × frequency resolution ≈ 1 ‖ 14/fft-spectral-analysis-windows
Laplace transform ‖ — ‖ Transform that turns linear differential equations into algebra in the complex frequency s; gives transfer functions, poles and zeros. ‖ F(s) = ∫₀^∞ f(t)e^{−st}dt; s = σ + jω ‖ 00/laplace-transfer-functions-bode
transfer function ‖ — ‖ Ratio of a linear system's output to its input as a function of complex frequency. Its poles and zeros set the frequency response and stability. ‖ H(s) = Y(s)/X(s) ‖ 00/laplace-transfer-functions-bode ‖ H(s)
pole ‖ — ‖ A value of s (or z) where a transfer function becomes infinite. Poles in the right half-plane (or outside the unit circle for z) mean an unstable system. ‖ each pole adds −20 dB/decade and up to −90° ‖ 00/laplace-transfer-functions-bode
zero ‖ — ‖ A value of s (or z) where a transfer function is zero. A right-half-plane zero (RHP zero) adds lag like a pole and makes feedback loops harder to stabilise. ‖ each zero adds +20 dB/decade ‖ 00/laplace-transfer-functions-bode ‖ RHP zero, RHP, LHP
Bode plot ‖ — ‖ Plots of a transfer function's magnitude (dB) and phase against log frequency; straight-line asymptotes make it quick to sketch. ‖ — ‖ 00/laplace-transfer-functions-bode
z-transform ‖ — ‖ The discrete-time counterpart of the Laplace transform; turns difference equations (digital filters, digital loops) into algebra in z. ‖ X(z) = Σ x[n]z^{−n}; stable if all poles have |z| < 1 ‖ 00/z-transform-and-discrete-time-systems
ROC ‖ region of convergence ‖ The set of s or z values for which a Laplace or z-transform sum converges; together with the poles it decides causality and stability. (On 21's sources page "ROC" is a page key for the rockets page.) ‖ causal + stable discrete system: ROC includes the unit circle ‖ 00/z-transform-and-discrete-time-systems
LTI ‖ linear time-invariant ‖ A system that obeys superposition and behaves the same at all times; fully described by its impulse response or transfer function. Filters, lines and antennas are LTI; mixers and amplifiers in compression are not. ‖ y = h * x ‖ 00/linear-systems-convolution
convolution ‖ — ‖ The operation that gives an LTI system's output from its input and impulse response; multiplication in the frequency domain. ‖ y(t) = ∫h(τ)x(t−τ)dτ ⇔ Y = HX ‖ 00/linear-systems-convolution
impulse response ‖ — ‖ Output of a linear system when the input is a very short spike; it fully characterises an LTI system. ‖ h(t) ⇔ H(f) ‖ 00/linear-systems-convolution
Kramers–Kronig relations ‖ — ‖ Causality forces the real and imaginary parts of a response (e.g. εr′ and εr″) to be linked: a material with loss must also show dispersion. ‖ — ‖ 00/linear-systems-convolution ‖ Kramers-Kronig
Hilbert transform ‖ — ‖ A 90° phase shift applied to every frequency; used to build the analytic signal (one-sided spectrum) and in image-reject mixers. ‖ — ‖ 14/iq-complex-baseband
PSD ‖ power spectral density ‖ How a signal's or noise's power is spread over frequency, in W/Hz or dBm/Hz. Integrate it over a band to get power. ‖ thermal noise PSD kT = −174 dBm/Hz at 290 K ‖ 00/probability-random-processes-and-psd
Wiener–Khinchin theorem ‖ — ‖ The PSD of a stationary random process is the Fourier transform of its autocorrelation. ‖ S(f) = F{R(τ)} ‖ 00/probability-random-processes-and-psd
autocorrelation ‖ — ‖ How similar a signal is to a time-shifted copy of itself. Sharp autocorrelation peaks make good radar waveforms and spreading codes. ‖ R(τ) = E[x(t)x*(t−τ)] ‖ 00/probability-random-processes-and-psd
Gaussian noise ‖ — ‖ Noise whose amplitude follows the normal (bell-curve) distribution; thermal noise is Gaussian. ‖ — ‖ 00/probability-random-processes-and-psd
AWGN ‖ additive white Gaussian noise ‖ The reference channel model: Gaussian noise with flat PSD simply added to the signal. Datasheet sensitivities and BER curves assume it. ‖ N0 = kT·F ‖ 10/ber-ebn0-and-shannon
Q-function ‖ Gaussian tail probability ‖ Probability that a standard normal variable exceeds x; it gives BER and false-alarm probabilities. ‖ BPSK BER = Q(√(2Eb/N0)); Q(4.75) ≈ 10⁻⁶ ‖ 00/probability-random-processes-and-psd ‖ erfc
Rayleigh distribution ‖ — ‖ Distribution of the envelope of complex Gaussian noise, and of a signal arriving by many paths with no dominant one (Rayleigh fading). ‖ margin vs mean power: 10/20/30 dB for 10/1/0.1 % outage ‖ 09/multipath-fading-doppler ‖ Rayleigh fading
Rician distribution ‖ — ‖ Envelope distribution of a steady (line-of-sight) component plus random scattered paths; the K-factor is the ratio of steady to scattered power. ‖ K = 0 → Rayleigh; large K → nearly Gaussian about the LOS level ‖ 09/multipath-fading-doppler ‖ Rician fading, Rice
Monte Carlo ‖ — ‖ Estimating a result by running many random trials (e.g. random noise, random tolerances) and averaging. Many KB numbers are "MC-verified". ‖ error falls as 1/√N trials ‖ 00/numerical-methods-overview ‖ MC
sinc ‖ — ‖ The function sin(πx)/(πx): the spectrum of a rectangular pulse, the pattern of a uniform aperture and the response of a DFT bin. ‖ first sidelobe −13.26 dB ‖ 00/special-functions-bessel-sinc
Bessel functions ‖ — ‖ Solutions of the cylindrical wave equation; they give FM sideband amplitudes, circular-waveguide and patch modes, and the jinc pattern of a circular aperture. ‖ FM carrier vanishes at β = 2.405 (J₀ zero) ‖ 00/special-functions-bessel-sinc ‖ Bessel, J_n
Chebyshev polynomial ‖ — ‖ Polynomials that ripple between ±1 then grow fast; they give equal-ripple filters and the Dolph–Chebyshev array taper. ‖ — ‖ 00/special-functions-bessel-sinc
least squares ‖ — ‖ Choosing model parameters to minimise the sum of squared residuals; used for calibration fits, GNSS position solutions and FIR design. ‖ x̂ = (HᵀH)⁻¹Hᵀy ‖ 00/estimation-theory-ml-map-crb ‖ LS, weighted least squares
ML ‖ maximum likelihood ‖ Estimation or detection that picks the parameter value (or symbol) that makes the observed data most probable. Also "mismatch loss" and "multilayer" on some pages — see the ambiguous-acronyms section. ‖ — ‖ 00/estimation-theory-ml-map-crb ‖ maximum likelihood
MAP ‖ maximum a posteriori ‖ Estimation or detection that also weighs prior knowledge (prior probabilities) with the likelihood. Equals ML when all outcomes are equally likely a priori. ‖ — ‖ 00/estimation-theory-ml-map-crb
MMSE ‖ minimum mean-square error ‖ Estimator that minimises the average squared error; for Gaussian problems it is the conditional mean, and the Kalman filter is its recursive form. ‖ — ‖ 00/estimation-theory-ml-map-crb
CRB ‖ Cramér–Rao bound ‖ The smallest variance any unbiased estimator can achieve, set by the Fisher information. Gives best-case accuracy for frequency, phase, delay and angle estimates. ‖ frequency CRB ∝ 1/(SNR·N³) ‖ 00/estimation-theory-ml-map-crb ‖ CRLB, Cramér–Rao lower bound
Fisher information ‖ — ‖ A measure of how sharply the likelihood depends on a parameter; its inverse is the Cramér–Rao bound. ‖ var(θ̂) ≥ 1/I(θ) ‖ 00/estimation-theory-ml-map-crb
Kalman filter ‖ — ‖ Recursive estimator that blends a motion model with noisy measurements, weighting each by its uncertainty; the standard tracker for radar and navigation. ‖ predict → update; gain K = PHᵀ(HPHᵀ+R)⁻¹ ‖ 00/kalman-filter-and-state-estimation ‖ KF
EKF ‖ extended Kalman filter ‖ Kalman filter for non-linear models, linearised about the current estimate at each step. ‖ — ‖ 00/kalman-filter-and-state-estimation
SVD ‖ singular value decomposition ‖ Factorisation of any matrix into rotations and scalings; reveals rank and conditioning, and underlies subspace methods such as MUSIC. ‖ A = UΣVᴴ ‖ 00/matrices-and-linear-algebra-for-networks
condition number ‖ — ‖ Ratio of a matrix's largest to smallest singular value; large values mean small measurement errors blow up in the solution (e.g. VNA calibration, GNSS geometry). ‖ κ = σ_max/σ_min ‖ 00/matrices-and-linear-algebra-for-networks
Newton–Raphson ‖ — ‖ Iterative root-finder that follows the local slope; used by SPICE and harmonic-balance solvers to converge non-linear circuits. ‖ x_{n+1} = x_n − f(x_n)/f′(x_n) ‖ 00/numerical-methods-overview ‖ Newton
ODE ‖ ordinary differential equation ‖ Equation linking a function of one variable to its derivatives; circuit transients are ODEs in time. ‖ RC: τ dv/dt + v = v_in ‖ 00/differential-equations-circuits-waves ‖ ODEs
PDE ‖ partial differential equation ‖ Equation in several variables (space and time); the wave equation and Maxwell's equations are PDEs. ‖ — ‖ 00/differential-equations-circuits-waves ‖ PDEs
curl ‖ — ‖ Vector derivative measuring local rotation of a field; Maxwell's equations say a changing magnetic field has an electric curl and vice versa. ‖ ∇×E = −∂B/∂t ‖ 00/vector-calculus-for-em ‖ divergence, gradient
# ---------------------------------------------------------------- circuit theory
impedance ‖ — ‖ The complex ratio of voltage to current at one frequency: resistance (real part) plus reactance (imaginary part), in ohms. ‖ Z = R + jX; Z_L = jωL, Z_C = 1/(jωC) ‖ 01/lumped-elements-impedance-admittance ‖ Z
admittance ‖ — ‖ The reciprocal of impedance: conductance plus susceptance, in siemens. Parallel elements add in admittance. ‖ Y = 1/Z = G + jB ‖ 01/lumped-elements-impedance-admittance ‖ Y
reactance ‖ — ‖ The imaginary part of impedance: energy is stored and returned rather than dissipated. Inductors have positive, capacitors negative reactance. ‖ X_L = ωL, X_C = −1/(ωC) ‖ 01/lumped-elements-impedance-admittance ‖ X
susceptance ‖ — ‖ The imaginary part of admittance. ‖ B_C = ωC ‖ 01/lumped-elements-impedance-admittance ‖ B
KCL ‖ Kirchhoff's current law ‖ The currents into any node sum to zero (charge is conserved). Valid only while the circuit is electrically small. ‖ ΣI = 0 ‖ 01/network-theorems-max-power-transfer
KVL ‖ Kirchhoff's voltage law ‖ The voltages around any closed loop sum to zero; strictly true only when no changing magnetic flux threads the loop (quasi-static). ‖ ΣV = 0 ‖ 02/quasi-static-vs-full-wave
MNA ‖ modified nodal analysis ‖ The matrix method SPICE uses to write circuit equations: node voltages plus currents in voltage sources and inductors. ‖ — ‖ 16/spice-circuit-simulation
Thévenin equivalent ‖ — ‖ Any linear two-terminal network behaves like one voltage source in series with one impedance (Norton: a current source in parallel). ‖ — ‖ 01/network-theorems-max-power-transfer ‖ Thevenin, Norton
maximum power transfer ‖ — ‖ A source delivers the most power to a load equal to the complex conjugate of its own impedance; only half the source's power then reaches the load. ‖ Z_L = Z_s* ; P_max = |V_s|²/(8R_s) (peak V) ‖ 01/network-theorems-max-power-transfer ‖ conjugate match
Q ‖ quality factor ‖ How sharply a resonator or reactive part stores energy compared with what it loses each cycle. High Q means narrow bandwidth and low loss. ‖ Q = 2π·(energy stored)/(energy lost per cycle) = f0/BW₃dB; inductor Q = X/R ‖ 01/resonance-and-q ‖ Q factor, Q-factor
unloaded Q ‖ — ‖ Q of a resonator counting only its own losses, before the source and load are attached. ‖ 1/Q_L = 1/Q_u + 1/Q_e ‖ 01/resonance-and-q ‖ Qu, Q_u
loaded Q ‖ — ‖ Q of a resonator including the source and load resistances; it sets the actual bandwidth. A resonator's insertion loss depends on the ratio of loaded to unloaded Q. ‖ IL = −20 log(1 − Q_L/Q_u) ‖ 01/resonance-and-q ‖ QL, Q_L
resonance ‖ — ‖ The frequency where inductive and capacitive reactances cancel, so a series circuit has minimum and a parallel circuit maximum impedance. ‖ f0 = 1/(2π√(LC)) ‖ 01/resonance-and-q
RLC ‖ resistor–inductor–capacitor ‖ The basic second-order circuit; its damping ratio and Q set ringing and bandwidth. ‖ ζ = 1/(2Q) ‖ 01/transient-and-step-response
time constant ‖ — ‖ The time a first-order circuit takes to cover 63 % of a step change. ‖ RC: τ = RC; RL: τ = L/R ‖ 01/transient-and-step-response ‖ τ, tau
rise time ‖ — ‖ Time for a step to go from 10 % to 90 %; tied to bandwidth for a first-order system. ‖ t_r ≈ 0.35/BW ‖ 01/transient-and-step-response
reactive power ‖ — ‖ Power that sloshes back and forth between source and reactive elements without being consumed; measured in volt-amperes reactive. ‖ Q = ½Im{VI*} (peak phasors) ‖ 01/energy-storage-and-reactive-power ‖ VAR
two-port ‖ — ‖ A network with an input and an output port, described by a 2×2 matrix (Z, Y, h, ABCD, S or T parameters). ‖ — ‖ 01/two-port-networks-zyh-abcd ‖ two-port network
ABCD parameters ‖ chain (transmission) parameters ‖ Two-port description relating input voltage and current to output voltage and current; cascaded stages simply multiply their ABCD matrices. ‖ [V1; I1] = [A B; C D][V2; I2] ‖ 01/two-port-networks-zyh-abcd ‖ ABCD
Z-parameters ‖ impedance parameters ‖ Two-port description giving port voltages from port currents (open-circuit measurements). ‖ V = Z·I ‖ 01/two-port-networks-zyh-abcd ‖ Z parameters
Y-parameters ‖ admittance parameters ‖ Two-port description giving port currents from port voltages (short-circuit measurements); natural for transistor models. ‖ I = Y·V ‖ 01/two-port-networks-zyh-abcd ‖ Y parameters
h-parameters ‖ hybrid parameters ‖ Mixed two-port description used for low-frequency BJT models (h_fe = current gain). ‖ — ‖ 01/two-port-networks-zyh-abcd
T-parameters ‖ transfer (scattering-transfer) parameters ‖ Rearranged S-parameters that, like ABCD, multiply for cascades; used in de-embedding. ‖ — ‖ 01/two-port-networks-zyh-abcd
LPF ‖ low-pass filter ‖ Filter that passes frequencies below its cut-off and attenuates those above; on a transmitter it removes harmonics. ‖ — ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes ‖ low-pass, LP filter
HPF ‖ high-pass filter ‖ Filter that passes frequencies above its cut-off. ‖ — ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes
BPF ‖ band-pass filter ‖ Filter that passes a band and rejects both sides; the pre-selector in front of an LNA or SDR. ‖ fractional bandwidth FBW = BW/f0 ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes ‖ band-pass
BSF ‖ band-stop (notch) filter ‖ Filter that rejects one band and passes the rest. ‖ — ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes ‖ notch, band-stop
FBW ‖ fractional bandwidth ‖ Bandwidth divided by centre frequency. Narrow FBW from lumped parts costs insertion loss because of finite component Q. ‖ FBW = (f₂ − f₁)/f0 ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes
low-pass prototype ‖ — ‖ A normalised (1 Ω, 1 rad/s) low-pass ladder whose element values gₖ are tabulated; scaling and transformation turn it into any LPF, HPF, BPF or BSF. ‖ — ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes ‖ g-values
Butterworth filter ‖ — ‖ Maximally flat passband response; moderate roll-off. ‖ −3 dB at cut-off; 20N dB/decade ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes ‖ Butterworth
Chebyshev filter ‖ — ‖ Equal ripple in the passband for a steeper roll-off than Butterworth of the same order. ‖ passband ripple ↔ return loss: RL = −10 log(1 − 10^{−Lr/10}) ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes ‖ Chebyshev
elliptic filter ‖ Cauer filter ‖ Ripple in both passband and stopband, with transmission zeros; the steepest transition for a given order. ‖ — ‖ 01/passive-filter-design-prototypes ‖ Cauer
Bessel filter ‖ Thomson filter ‖ Low-pass with maximally flat group delay, so pulses keep their shape; slow roll-off. ‖ — ‖ 05/active-filters
group delay ‖ — ‖ How long each frequency's envelope takes to pass through a network. Non-flat group delay smears pulses and chirps. ‖ τ_g = −dφ/dω ‖ 01/resonance-and-q
transformer ‖ — ‖ Coupled windings that transform voltage, current and impedance by the turns ratio (and can isolate or balance). ‖ Z_in = n²Z_L for turns ratio n ‖ 01/transformers-coupled-inductors
coupling coefficient ‖ — ‖ Fraction of one coil's flux that links another, between 0 and 1. ‖ M = k√(L₁L₂) ‖ 01/transformers-coupled-inductors ‖ k
mutual inductance ‖ — ‖ Voltage induced in one circuit per rate of change of current in another. ‖ v₂ = M di₁/dt ‖ 02/inductance-capacitance-from-fields ‖ M
partial inductance ‖ — ‖ Inductance assigned to a segment of a current loop (e.g. one via) by convention; only the whole loop's inductance is physical, so every via or trace figure must say where its return path is. ‖ — ‖ 02/inductance-capacitance-from-fields ‖ loop inductance
# ---------------------------------------------------------------- electromagnetics
Maxwell's equations ‖ — ‖ The four laws of electromagnetism: charges make electric fields, there are no magnetic charges, a changing magnetic field induces an electric field, and currents plus changing electric fields make magnetic fields. Everything in the KB follows from them. ‖ ∇×E = −jωB; ∇×H = J + jωD (phasor form) ‖ 02/maxwells-equations
displacement current ‖ — ‖ The term ∂D/∂t in Ampère's law: a changing electric field acts like a current. It lets current "flow" through a capacitor and makes radiation possible. ‖ I_D = C dV/dt ‖ 02/maxwells-equations
plane wave ‖ — ‖ A wave whose fields are uniform over flat planes perpendicular to its direction; the far field of any antenna looks locally like one. ‖ E/H = η; η0 = 376.73 Ω in free space ‖ 02/wave-equation-plane-waves-polarization
η0 ‖ intrinsic impedance of free space ‖ The ratio of electric to magnetic field in a plane wave in vacuum. ‖ η0 = √(μ0/ε0) = 376.730 Ω (120π = 376.99 Ω is an acceptable hand value) ‖ 02/electromagnetic-units-and-constants ‖ eta0, wave impedance
wavelength ‖ — ‖ Distance a wave travels in one cycle; in a medium it shrinks by √εr. ‖ λ = c/f; 433 MHz → 69.2 cm; 915 MHz → 32.8 cm; 1575.42 MHz → 19.0 cm ‖ 02/wave-equation-plane-waves-polarization ‖ λ, lambda

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- transmission lines and matching
transmission line ‖ — ‖ A pair of conductors (coax, microstrip, twisted pair) treated as distributed inductance and capacitance, so signals travel as waves with a characteristic impedance. ‖ v = 1/√(L′C′) ‖ 03/telegrapher-equations-characteristic-impedance ‖ TL
telegrapher's equations ‖ — ‖ The pair of differential equations for voltage and current on a transmission line, built from per-metre R, L, G and C. ‖ dV/dz = −(R′+jωL′)I; dI/dz = −(G′+jωC′)V ‖ 03/telegrapher-equations-characteristic-impedance ‖ telegrapher equations
RLGC ‖ per-unit-length resistance, inductance, conductance, capacitance ‖ The four line constants of the telegrapher model; field solvers output them. ‖ Z0 = √((R′+jωL′)/(G′+jωC′)) ‖ 03/telegrapher-equations-characteristic-impedance
Z0 ‖ characteristic impedance ‖ The voltage-to-current ratio of a wave travelling one way on a line; a line terminated in Z0 shows no reflection. ‖ lossless Z0 = √(L′/C′); RF default 50 Ω ‖ 03/telegrapher-equations-characteristic-impedance ‖ characteristic impedance
50 Ω ‖ fifty-ohm reference impedance ‖ The standard RF system impedance: a compromise between the lowest-loss (≈ 77 Ω) and highest-power (≈ 30 Ω) air coax. S-parameters, Γ and dBm-to-voltage conversions in the KB are referenced to 50 Ω. ‖ 0 dBm in 50 Ω = 0.224 V rms ‖ 03/coax-and-cables ‖ 50 ohm, 50-ohm
velocity factor ‖ — ‖ Speed of a wave on a cable as a fraction of the speed of light; it shortens the physical length of λ/4 stubs and baluns. ‖ VF = 1/√εr: solid PE 0.66, PTFE ≈ 0.69–0.70, foam ≈ 0.8 ‖ 03/coax-and-cables ‖ VF
electrical length ‖ — ‖ Length of a line measured in wavelengths or degrees of phase at the operating frequency. ‖ θ = βℓ = 360°·ℓ/λ_g ‖ 03/input-impedance-stubs-quarter-wave
Γ ‖ reflection coefficient ‖ Ratio of reflected to incident wave voltage at a port or load; 0 for a perfect match, magnitude 1 for an open or short. ‖ Γ = (Z_L − Z0)/(Z_L + Z0) ‖ 03/reflections-vswr-return-loss-mismatch ‖ reflection coefficient, Gamma, rho
VSWR ‖ voltage standing-wave ratio ‖ Ratio of maximum to minimum voltage along a mismatched line; a simple scalar measure of match. ‖ VSWR = (1+|Γ|)/(1−|Γ|); 2:1 ↔ |Γ| = 0.33, RL 9.5 dB, 0.51 dB mismatch loss ‖ 03/reflections-vswr-return-loss-mismatch ‖ SWR
RL ‖ return loss ‖ How far below the incident power the reflected power is, as a positive dB number; bigger is better. mismatch loss ‖ — ‖ The fraction of available power not delivered because part of it is reflected. ‖ ML = −10 log₁₀(1 − |Γ|²); |Γ| = 0.1 → 0.04 dB ‖ 03/reflections-vswr-return-loss-mismatch

S-parameters ‖ scattering parameters ‖ Two-port (or N-port) description in terms of incident and reflected waves at matched ports — what a VNA measures. # ---------------------------------------------------------------- propagation and link budgets
FSPL ‖ free-space path loss ‖ The spreading loss between two isotropic antennas in empty space. It grows with frequency only because a fixed-gain antenna's aperture shrinks. ‖ FSPL = 32.45 + 20 log f_MHz + 20 log d_km dB (433 MHz, 10 km: 105.2 dB) ‖ 09/friis-free-space-path-loss ‖ free-space path loss
Friis transmission equation ‖ — ‖ Received power between two antennas in free space from transmit power, both gains and the wavelength. ‖ P_r = P_tG_tG_r(λ/4πd)² ‖ 09/friis-free-space-path-loss ‖ Friis equation
EIRP ‖ effective isotropic radiated power ‖ Transmit power plus antenna gain (minus feed loss) in a direction: the power an isotropic antenna would need to give the same field. ‖ EIRP = P_t − L_feed + G_t (dBm, dBi) ‖ 09/link-budget-methodology ‖ e.i.r.p.
ERP ‖ effective radiated power ‖ Like EIRP but referenced to a half-wave dipole instead of an isotrope; many regulations (incl. Singapore's SRD rules) use it. ‖ ERP = EIRP − 2.15 dB ‖ 09/link-budget-methodology, 00/canonical-values-and-conventions ‖ e.r.p.
link budget ‖ — ‖ A line-by-line tally of gains and losses from transmitter to receiver, ending in received power and margin over sensitivity. ‖ P_r = EIRP − L_path − L_misc + G_r ‖ 09/link-budget-methodology ‖ link-budget
link margin ‖ — ‖ How many dB the received power exceeds the receiver's sensitivity; the allowance for fading, pointing and polarization losses. ‖ margin = P_r − S ‖ 09/link-margin-availability ‖ margin
fade margin ‖ — ‖ Extra margin reserved for fading so that a link stays up for a stated fraction of the time. ‖ Rayleigh, vs mean power: 10/20/30 dB for 90/99/99.9 % ‖ 09/link-margin-availability
availability ‖ — ‖ Fraction of time (or of locations) a link meets its required SNR. ‖ — ‖ 09/link-margin-availability

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- SDR and DSP
SDR ‖ software-defined radio ‖ A radio whose channel filtering, demodulation and often mixing are done in software on digitised samples; the hardware is a tunable front end plus ADC/DAC. ‖ — ‖ 14/sdr-hardware-survey ‖ SDRs, software-defined radio
IQ ‖ in-phase and quadrature ‖ The pair of baseband signals (cosine and sine components) that together represent a band-pass signal as a complex number, so positive and negative frequencies can be told apart. ‖ x(t) = I(t) + jQ(t); s(t) = Re{x(t)e^{j2πf_ct}} ‖ 14/iq-complex-baseband ‖ I/Q, I and Q, complex baseband
analytic signal ‖ — ‖ A complex signal with only positive frequencies, made from a real signal plus j times its Hilbert transform. ‖ — ‖ 14/iq-complex-baseband
IQ imbalance ‖ — ‖ Gain and phase mismatch between the I and Q paths; creates an image of each signal mirrored about the centre frequency. ‖ IRR ≈ 40 dB for 1° and 0.1 dB ‖ 14/iq-complex-baseband ‖ I/Q imbalance
DC spike ‖ — ‖ A spur at 0 Hz (the tuned centre) of a zero-IF SDR's spectrum, from LO leakage and DC offsets; offset tuning moves the wanted signal away from it. ‖ — ‖ 14/iq-complex-baseband ‖ DC offset, LO leakage, offset tuning
Nyquist rate ‖ — ‖ Sampling must exceed twice the highest frequency (real samples) or the bandwidth (complex samples) to avoid aliasing. ‖ real: f_s > 2B; complex: f_s > B ‖ 14/sampling-aliasing-bandpass-sampling ‖ Nyquist, Nyquist frequency
aliasing ‖ — ‖ Frequencies above half the sample rate folding back into the band, where they cannot be distinguished from real signals. ‖ alias at |f − kf_s| ‖ 14/sampling-aliasing-bandpass-sampling ‖ alias
bandpass sampling ‖ undersampling ‖ Deliberately sampling a narrow band-pass signal below twice its carrier, using aliasing to shift it to baseband; needs a good band-pass filter. ‖ — ‖ 14/sampling-aliasing-bandpass-sampling ‖ undersampling, Nyquist zones
Nyquist zone ‖ — ‖ Each fs/2-wide band of input frequencies that fold onto the same output band of a sampler. ‖ zone n spans (n−1)fs/2 … nfs/2 ‖ 14/sampling-aliasing-bandpass-sampling ‖ Nyquist zones
FIR ‖ finite impulse response filter ‖ Digital filter whose output is a weighted sum of recent inputs only; always stable and can have exactly linear phase. ‖ taps ≈ (A_dB/22)·(f_s/Δf) (Harris rule) ‖ 14/digital-filters-fir-iir ‖ FIR filter, FIRs
IIR ‖ infinite impulse response filter ‖ Digital filter with feedback; fewer coefficients than FIR for a sharp response, but non-linear phase and possible instability. ‖ — ‖ 14/digital-filters-fir-iir ‖ IIR filter, biquad
Parks–McClellan ‖ Remez exchange FIR design ‖ Algorithm for equiripple optimal FIR filters. ‖ — ‖ 14/digital-filters-fir-iir ‖ Remez, equiripple
bilinear transform ‖ — ‖ Mapping an analog filter to a digital IIR filter by substituting for s; warps the frequency axis, so critical frequencies are pre-warped. ‖ s = (2/T)(z − 1)/(z + 1) ‖ 14/digital-filters-fir-iir
decimation ‖ — ‖ Low-pass filtering then keeping every M-th sample, to reduce sample rate. ‖ — ‖ 14/multirate-dsp-decimation-cic
interpolation ‖ — ‖ Raising the sample rate by inserting samples and filtering out images. ‖ — ‖ 14/multirate-dsp-decimation-cic ‖ upsampling
CIC ‖ cascaded integrator–comb filter ‖ Multiplier-free decimation/interpolation filter of integrators and combs; cheap for large rate changes but droops, so it is followed by a compensating FIR. ‖ N-stage response (sinc)^N ‖ 14/multirate-dsp-decimation-cic ‖ CIC filter
half-band filter ‖ — ‖ FIR filter with its cut-off at a quarter of the sample rate, so nearly half its taps are zero; efficient for ÷2 decimation stages. ‖ — ‖ 14/multirate-dsp-decimation-cic ‖ HB filter, HB1, HB2, HB3
polyphase filter ‖ — ‖ An FIR split into M sub-filters so that only the samples that survive decimation are computed; also the basis of channelisers. ‖ — ‖ 14/multirate-dsp-decimation-cic ‖ polyphase, channeliser
NCO ‖ numerically controlled oscillator ‖ Digital oscillator made from a phase accumulator and a sine lookup (or CORDIC); used for digital mixing in DDCs and DUCs. ‖ resolution f_clk/2^N ‖ 14/nco-ddc-duc
DDC ‖ digital down-converter ‖ NCO mixer plus decimating filters that select one channel from a wideband ADC stream and bring it to complex baseband. ‖ — ‖ 14/nco-ddc-duc ‖ DDCs, digital down-conversion
DUC ‖ digital up-converter ‖ Interpolating filters plus NCO mixer that move a baseband signal up to an IF for a DAC. ‖ — ‖ 14/nco-ddc-duc
CORDIC ‖ coordinate rotation digital computer ‖ Shift-and-add algorithm for rotations, sines/cosines and magnitudes; used in FPGA NCOs and demodulators. ‖ ≈ 1 bit of accuracy per iteration ‖ 14/nco-ddc-duc
window function ‖ — ‖ Taper applied to a block of samples before an FFT to reduce spectral leakage, trading resolution and noise bandwidth. ‖ Hann ENBW 1.50 bins, scallop 1.42 dB; Hamming 1.36 bins, 1.75 dB; Blackman 1.73 bins ‖ 14/fft-spectral-analysis-windows ‖ window, Hann, Hamming, Blackman, Kaiser

scalloping loss ‖ — ‖ Drop in an FFT's reading of a tone that falls midway between bins. ‖ Hann 1.42 dB; rectangular 3.92 dB ‖ 14/fft-spectral-analysis-windows ‖ scallop loss

LoRa ‖ Long Range (Semtech's chirp modulation) ‖ Semtech's proprietary chirp-spread-spectrum physical layer; trades data rate for sensitivity, receiving below the noise floor. The KB's telemetry link. ‖ R_b = SF·(BW/2^SF)·4/(4+CR) ‖ 15/lora-phy-css-math
LoRaWAN ‖ LoRa wide-area network ‖ The network protocol (MAC, gateways, servers, security) built on LoRa for IoT; usually not what a rocket link needs. ‖ — ‖ 15/lorawan-vs-raw-lora ‖ LoRa WAN
SF ‖ spreading factor ‖ LoRa's chips-per-symbol exponent (SF5–SF12): each symbol is a chirp of 2^SF chips carrying SF bits. One SF step doubles time on air for ≈ 2.5 dB more sensitivity. (SF is also a page key and a VNA error term — see ambiguous acronyms.) ‖ T_sym = 2^SF/BW; SNR_req −7.5 dB (SF7) … −20 dB (SF12) ‖ 15/lora-phy-css-math ‖ SF7, SF9, SF10, SF11, SF12, SF5, SF6, SF8, spreading factor
BW (LoRa) ‖ LoRa bandwidth ‖ The chirp's swept bandwidth (7.8–500 kHz; 125 kHz is common); it equals the chip rate. ‖ BW125 = 125 kHz ‖ 15/lora-phy-css-math ‖ BW125, BW250, BW500, BW

GNSS ‖ global navigation satellite system ‖ Generic name for satellite positioning systems (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou, plus regional QZSS and NavIC). ‖ signals at L-band 1.16–1.61 GHz, ≈ −128.5 dBm at the ground ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ GNSS receiver
GPS ‖ Global Positioning System ‖ The US GNSS. L1 C/A at 1575.42 MHz is the signal every receiver uses first. ‖ L1 C/A: BPSK, 1023 chips at 1.023 Mcps, 50 b/s; minimum −128.5 dBm ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ NAVSTAR

Galileo ‖ — ‖ The European GNSS, with E1 (1575.42 MHz, CBOC) and E5a/E5b signals and the free High Accuracy Service. ‖ — ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ E1, E5a, E5b, E6, HAS
BeiDou ‖ BDS ‖ China's GNSS (BDS-3), with B1I, B1C and B2a signals; many satellites visible from Singapore. ‖ B1I 1561.098 MHz ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ BDS, BDS-3, B1I, B1C, B2a
QZSS ‖ Quasi-Zenith Satellite System ‖ Japan's regional GNSS augmentation, with GPS-compatible signals and the CLAS/MADOCA correction services. ‖ — ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ CLAS, MADOCA
NavIC ‖ Navigation with Indian Constellation ‖ India's regional navigation system (formerly IRNSS). ‖ — ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ IRNSS
SBAS ‖ satellite-based augmentation system ‖ Geostationary satellites broadcasting GPS corrections and integrity data (WAAS, EGNOS, MSAS, GAGAN); metre-level improvement. ‖ — ‖ 21/rtk-ppp-and-corrections ‖ WAAS, EGNOS
L1 ‖ GPS L1 band ‖ The 1575.42 MHz GNSS band (GPS L1, Galileo E1, BeiDou B1C, QZSS). L2 = 1227.60 MHz, L5 = 1176.45 MHz. ‖ λ ≈ 19.0 cm ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ L2, L5, L1C, L2C, L1 C/A
C/A code ‖ coarse/acquisition code ‖ GPS's civil ranging code on L1: a 1023-chip Gold code repeating every 1 ms. ‖ one chip ≈ 293 m ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ C/A
BOC ‖ binary offset carrier ‖ GNSS modulation that multiplies the code by a square-wave subcarrier, splitting the spectrum into two lobes and sharpening the correlation peak (CBOC, TMBOC, AltBOC are variants). ‖ BOC(1,1) ‖ 21/gnss-signals-and-codes ‖ CBOC, TMBOC, AltBOC
navigation message ‖ — ‖ The slow data stream on a GNSS signal carrying time, clock corrections, ephemeris and almanac. ‖ GPS LNAV 50 b/s; subframes 1–3 take 18 s of a 30 s frame ‖ 21/ttff-agps-almanac-ephemeris ‖ LNAV, nav message

almanac ‖ — ‖ Coarse orbits of the whole constellation, valid for weeks; only speeds up the satellite search. ‖ full GPS almanac takes 12.5 min ‖ 21/ttff-agps-almanac-ephemeris
TTFF ‖ time to first fix ‖ Time from power-on (or reset) to the first position solution: cold start (no data), warm (almanac and time), hot (valid ephemeris). ‖ ZED-F9P cold 25–30 s, hot 2 s; MAX-M10S cold 23–28 s (multi-GNSS), hot 1 s ‖ 21/ttff-agps-almanac-ephemeris ‖ cold start, warm start, hot start

acquisition ‖ — ‖ The GNSS receiver's search over code phase and Doppler to find each satellite's signal before tracking it. ‖ — ‖ 21/gnss-receiver-architecture-acquisition-tracking
tracking loops ‖ — ‖ The DLL (code), PLL (carrier phase) and FLL (frequency) feedback loops that follow each satellite after acquisition. Constant acceleration costs nothing in steady state; jerk breaks lock. ‖ — ‖ 21/gnss-receiver-architecture-acquisition-tracking ‖ tracking
DLL ‖ delay-locked loop ‖ The loop that keeps a receiver's code replica aligned with the incoming code using early, prompt and late correlators. ‖ — ‖ 21/gnss-receiver-architecture-acquisition-tracking ‖ early–late

pseudorange ‖ — ‖ Measured transmit-to-receive time times c, including the receiver clock error ("pseudo" because of that clock bias). Four satellites solve x, y, z and clock. ‖ — ‖ 21/gnss-error-sources-dop
carrier phase ‖ — ‖ GNSS measurement of the received carrier's accumulated phase; mm-level precise but with an unknown whole number of cycles (the ambiguity). ‖ — ‖ 21/rtk-ppp-and-corrections ‖ carrier-phase

RTCM ‖ Radio Technical Commission for Maritime Services (RTCM 3 messages) ‖ The standard format for GNSS correction messages (1005 base position, MSM4/MSM7 observations, 1230 GLONASS biases). ‖ 4-constellation dual-frequency MSM4 at 1 Hz ≈ 710 B/s (MSM7 ≈ 1130 B/s) ‖ 21/rtk-ppp-and-corrections ‖ RTCM3, MSM, MSM4, MSM7
