## 4. Misconceptions and knowledge gaps revealed by the inconsistencies

1. **Single- vs double-sideband bookkeeping in phase noise (#1).** The 05 page treats the two noise sidebands as adding to the
   SSB quantity. The correct chain: additive noise splits equally into AM and PM; 𝓛 is one sideband of the PM, so 𝓛 = S_φ/2. "P_s" is also
   defined differently by Leeson and Rubiola (amplifier input) and by Lee and Hajimiri (power dissipated in the tank). *Gap:* the KB has no
   derivation that maps one definition onto the other for a concrete circuit (e.g. a Colpitts with a known tank R). It also lacks the ISF/LTV
   treatment (D5's rank-1 gap). Lee–Hajimiri's equations were read only as surrounding text (they are images in the PDF), so their equation
   numbers should be re-checked from a rendered copy.
2. **The "3 dB convention" traps cluster.** #1 (SSB vs S_φ), #8 (2E/N0 vs E/N0), #15 (real vs complex sampling), and the already-
   documented peak vs RMS phasors, dBi vs dBic (3 dB linear-to-CP), mean vs median fading, and ERP vs EIRP (2.15 dB) are all
   factor-2 conventions. Each page is right in its own convention, but the KB has no single place that lists them. **Recommendation:** add a
   "Factor-of-2 and 3 dB traps" section to `00-math-foundations/decibels-and-log-units.md` that links each convention to its canonical choice.
3. **Inductance without a return path (#7).** The Johnson & Graham "+1" via formula and the "≈1 nH per via" folklore persist because
   people quote partial inductance as if it were a circuit property. 02 explains loop vs partial inductance well, but 03 still uses the folklore.
   Rule for the KB: every via or trace inductance number states its return (plane below, return via at distance s, or isolated).
4. **Closed-form vs fab-calculator widths (#11, #12).** Disagreements of 3–7 % come from model inputs (mask model, trapezoid, dielectric
   thickness, εr), not from formula accuracy. *Gap:* the KB has no authoritative JLC 2-layer stackup thickness and no TDR measurement of a JLC
   coupon (W1-A4 already flagged this). The width that goes on the order must come from the fab's calculator for the named stackup.
5. **Data-sheet hygiene (#13, and the EBYTE sensitivity).** Feature-list and marketing figures (BGA524 "−12 dBm", EBYTE "−147 dBm") get
   copied in preference to the electrical-characteristics table. Rule: quote the electrical table at its stated conditions, and record the
   discrepancy when the vendor contradicts itself.
6. **Scenario numbers presented as general facts (#14, #16, #19).** "Landed range ≈ 5.6 km", "C/N0 ≈ 42 dB-Hz typical" and "RTL NF ≈ 7 dB"
   are true only for one set of assumptions, and other pages give different numbers under different assumptions. Duplicate topics (link budgets
   in 09/15/20, NF in 07/14/17/20, rockets in 08/15/20/21) should each have **one canonical scenario table** that the others cite instead of
   recomputing.
7. **Classic tables can mix variants (#9).** harris 1978's Hamming 1.78 dB is the scalloping of the 25/46 window, while his ENBW and PSL
   are 0.54-window values. Rule: recompute classic table entries rather than calling them misprints or copying them.
8. **Envelope limits vs mechanisms (#22, #17).** "4 g" is a u-blox operational envelope; the physical cause of boost dropouts is jerk and
   oscillator g-sensitivity. The class-F "only a 3rd harmonic" wording hides the infinite-even-harmonic assumption. In both cases the KB states
