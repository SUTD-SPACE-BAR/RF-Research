## 0. Method (short)

- **Access (what worked and what did not).** EEVblog and QRZ sit behind Cloudflare/CrowdSec and return HTTP 403 to
  `curl` and to WebFetch. Their thread titles therefore come from **WebSearch result titles** (`site:` queries, which
  give exact page titles but no reply or view counts), plus four **Wayback Machine** snapshots of the EEVblog RF board
  index (2021-01, 2022-01, 2023-12, 2026-08), which do carry counts. QRZ is barely indexed (about one hit per
  `site:` query), so it contributes only 7 rows. groups.io topic pages need JavaScript and its RSS shows only the
  latest 20 messages, so nanovna-users, tinysa and LibreVNA topics also come from WebSearch. RadioReference returns
  405 and eHam's forums moved, so both come from WebSearch too (eHam rows are articles plus their comment threads).
  These raw sources were read directly, all read-only:
  - Discourse `search.json` for the **TTN forum**, the **SOTA reflector** and the **Arduino forum** (reply counts).
  - **Meshtastic GitHub Discussions** through the read-only GraphQL search API (comment and upvote counts).
  - **Hackaday** tag and category RSS feeds (comment counts), plus the comment threads of 30 posts.
  - **YouTube** channel "Popular" lists and topic-search result pages, parsed from the page's initial-data JSON
    (exact titles and view counts), plus the top-40 comments of 20 flagship explainers.
  The Meshtastic Discourse is gone (403/000). No logins, posting or voting.
- **What was harvested (≈ 6 300 titles).**
  - Forums: TTN 995 topics (36 queries); SOTA 1 088 (33); Arduino 1 351 (23); Meshtastic Discussions 299 (22).
  - Hackaday: 304 posts from 18 feeds, with the comments of 30 of them.
  - YouTube: 967 popular videos from 17 channels, including W2AEW, Keysight/Keysight Labs, Tektronix, Rohde & Schwarz,
    Andreas Spiess, Tech Minds, Phil's Lab, Robert Feranec, The Signal Path, Mr Carlson's Lab, All About Circuits and
    **HB9BLA (the channel exists: 32 videos)**, plus IMSAI Guy, KB9VBR, Fesz, EEVblog and Ham Radio Crash Course. A
    further 969 results came from 54 topic searches.
  - WebSearch: 228 titles from EEVblog, groups.io, RadioReference, eHam and QRZ.
  - Wayback: 119 EEVblog topics.
  - Not reachable: YouTube transcripts (the timed-text endpoint needs signed parameters). YouTube rows are therefore
    based on titles and descriptions plus machine-filtered top comments. The top comments turned out to be mostly
    praise, with few questions.
- **Selection.** On-topic titles were picked by RF keyword filters and popularity, then read by hand. Dropped:
  firmware/app bugs, operating chit-chat, product launches, restorations and audio. Hackaday comment threads were
  read for recurring questions and objections, which are kept as separate rows ("Hackaday comment threads"). These
  are paraphrased; no comment text is reproduced. **699 questions were classified (664 distinct threads, posts or
  videos).**
- **Coverage check.** Each row was compared with FAQ.md, the live "Questions this page answers" lists, a regex search
  over kb/ (`kbg.py`) and, when unsure, the page section itself. Status definitions follow D1: **covered** = a page
  answers this specific question; **partial** = the topic is there but not the specific point, practical step or
  myth; **missing** = nothing usable.
- **Re-checks.** Wave 5 kept writing pages during the run (KB grew 195 → 236 pages). These pages landed and were
  used: 08/inductive-coupling-rfid-nfc-wpt; 08/wire-antennas-hf-random-wire-fan-dipole; four new 13 pages
  (rf-pcb-review-checklist, thermal-management-rf, mixed-signal-layout, jlcpcb-rf-manufacturing);
  14/sdr-front-end-protection-and-filtering; 14/signal-identification-and-spectrum-survey; 20/patch-antenna-,
  lna-design- and gnss-front-end-design walkthroughs; and nine 21-gnss pages. Rows were re-assessed twice
  (2026-10-02): 53 rows changed. 46 were upgraded because of new pages, 2 were downgraded at spot-check and 5 got
  note-only changes.
- **Spot-check of "covered".** 16 covered rows were re-read against page text: 2 were downgraded (wideband LNA #56,
  MeshCore #458) and 2 partial rows were found to be covered (underwater, #311/#423). The residual over-call rate is
  therefore about 10 %, consistent with D1/D2.
- **Popularity.** The units differ by source: replies (Discourse/EEVblog), comments (Hackaday/GitHub), views
  (YouTube), or "—" (WebSearch-only). For ranking, Σpop = Σ(replies + comments) + Σ(views)/1000.
