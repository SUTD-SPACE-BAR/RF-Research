- 2026-10-03 — DF: K0OV offset attenuator (homingin.com/joek0ov/offatten.html, 73 Magazine May 1998; curl -k needed, cert expired): mixes signal with a 4 MHz LO in a diode, receiver tuned ±4 MHz; on-frequency ≈ 4–20 dB, offset path −30 dB to >100 dB; passive step attenuators fail on foot because HT cases leak; does not protect the receiver front end; Baofeng-type SDR-architecture HTs produce spurs/desense within ~15–20 ft of a 50 mW fox → wrong bearings; avoid transmitting through it. Body fade: hold receiver at the abdomen, turn; the minimum points the source *behind* you (body attenuates, not reflects); disconnect the antenna when close (Barrie ARC / Moell & Curlee "Transmitter Hunting" TAB 1987 is the book source). Body-blocking loss: body-centric channel reviews report up to ~20 dB (PMC4579845) — treat 10–20 dB as estimate.
  Pseudo-Doppler: Gerhard & Tokekar arXiv:2003.00386 (2020, preprint) — HackRF + Operacake 4-element, spacing ≤ λ/2 (square ≤ 0.35λ side), ~330 Hz bearing rate, particle-filter localisation 4.6–22 m; theory φ = (2πR/λ)cos(ω_r t − α). Own derivation to write: peak deviation Δf = 2πR f_r/λ; noise-limited σ_α ≈ λ/(πD√(K·SNR)) (matches a search-snippet formula 2·10^(−SNR/20)·90λ/(π²D) deg for K = 1) — verify by Monte Carlo. Adcock/Watson–Watt octantal error derived: δθ ≈ (πd/λ)²/24 · sin4θ rad (d = λ/2 → 5.9° max) — verify numerically. Peak-reading: 12(δθ/θ3)² dB ⇒ 1 dB resolution → ±0.29θ3; −3 dB-edge bisect slope 12/θ3 dB/° ⇒ much finer.
